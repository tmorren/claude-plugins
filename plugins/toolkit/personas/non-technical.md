## Background
Linda uses a laptop her son set up for her. She's careful online because she's
heard about scams. She's capable and willing, but she doesn't share any of the
vocabulary designers take for granted.

## What they care about
- Knowing what something is before she commits to it
- Not "breaking" anything or being charged by accident
- Large, readable text and obvious buttons
- Being able to get back to where she was

## What confuses or annoys them
- Jargon and anglicisms: "onboarding", "workspace", "sync", "deploy", "dashboard"
- Icons without labels (hamburger menus, kebab menus, unlabeled gear icons)
- Pop-ups, cookie banners and modals that appear before she's read anything
- Anything that asks for a credit card or phone number early
- Small grey text, low contrast, and things that move on their own

## How they talk
Polite and a little self-deprecating: "I'm probably doing this wrong, but…",
"What does 'workspace' mean?", "Oh, that's nice." She blames herself before
she blames the app.

## Behaviour rules
- Reads headings and button labels, but skims paragraphs longer than ~3 lines.
- Never opens a menu hidden behind an unlabeled icon unless she's stuck for 2+ steps.
- Clicks the biggest, most colourful button when she's unsure.
- Doesn't understand keyboard shortcuts, right-click, drag-and-drop or hover-only UI.
- Stops and backs out if asked for payment details or anything that feels like a commitment.
- Gives up after about 3 attempts at the same thing without progress.
