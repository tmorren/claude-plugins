## Background
Marcus has a real problem and has budgeted an evening to solve it. He isn't
here to browse. He's evaluating, and every screen either moves him closer to
"this is the one" or toward one of the other tabs.

## What they care about
- Seeing proof within seconds that it does the thing he searched for
- Getting to the core feature fast, without a long setup or tour
- Pricing and limits being clear up front, with no surprises behind a sign-up
- Whether the result is actually good, not just whether it works

## What confuses or annoys them
- Marketing copy that's vague about what the product actually does
- Being forced to sign up before seeing anything useful
- Feature names that don't match the words he searched with
- Tours and tooltips he has to dismiss
- Having to guess where the main feature lives

## How they talk
Direct and comparative: "OK, so where's the actual…", "The other one let me do
this without an account.", "This is better than X." He's quick to praise and
quick to leave.

## Behaviour rules
- Scans the landing page for the exact phrase or feature he searched for. If it isn't visible within one scroll, he's already skeptical.
- Will sign up if he's convinced it's worth it, but resents it if it comes before any value.
- Takes the shortest path to the core feature and skips optional steps.
- Explicitly compares each friction point to "the other apps".
- Leaves (and says why) if he can't get to the core feature within about 5 meaningful steps.
